# Klarify

## Overview
Klarify is an AI Copilot to support both practitioners and clients on the therapeutic journey. My work as lead designer was to spearhead the design initiative for this stealth startup. This included designing and developing the website, screens for the web app, and corresponding mobile app design. Over the course of 4 months, hundreds of screens were designed and iterated on, based on feedback from the primary audience: therapists. The design philosophy and goal behind all of the designs was to focus on saving therapists time, so that they can spend their efforts in supporting their clients. This resulted in stripping back everything that could be stripped back, and prioritizing heavily on the user experience (UX) with a focus on how everything could be done simpler. Another key factor here was that AI was baked into every part of the design, as the entire backend is reliant on LLMs to produce their outputs. 

## Features
* A built in LLM feature that therapists can interact with.
* A recording page and feature, used by therapists to record their sessions.
* A custom template creation flow.
* A fully built billing and admin management experience.
* A client-side of the app.
* A detailed overview page used to create session notes, and track client progress.

## Challenges
- **Problem:** Integrating confusing technology into an app primarily catered towards non-tech savvy users such as therapists presented a lot of difficulties. User interviews would often result in therapists not understanding what was going on, how their data was being used, or how to interact with AI features.
- **Solution:** To refer to the core design philosphy of *less is more.* By recentering the designs around that one ideology, as well as frequently running user interviews or collecting feedback from actual users, major problems were able to be fixed before being pushed to production.

## Learnings
- One of the biggest learnings from this contract was to apply a systems-level thinking to every feature. Early on, I frequently found myself designing a feature or flow without taking into consideration how this would fit into the overall flow of things, or match the design language. This resulted in countless iterations, as different features were designed individually and then brought together by creating a unified design system. However, had I applied this type of thinking earlier on in the project, it likely would have saved time and made the entire process more efficient.
